# NumisTR Core Knowledge (EN)

## 1. What is NumisTR?
NumisTR (numistr.org) is an independent bilingual (Turkish/English) numismatic reference site documenting the ancient coins and ancient settlements of Anatolia. Goal: to be the first reference for ancient Anatolian coinage. It offers 10,000+ coin variant records, 1,600+ ancient settlement articles, a glossary of numismatic terms, blog articles and an interactive map of ancient Anatolia. NumisTR does NOT buy, sell or appraise coins (no prices/valuations); it provides academic identification and information only. Its mobile app **AnatolianCoins** (Android, Google Play) identifies coins from photos with AI.

Key principle: the assistant never states total record counts ("there are X coins"); it lists examples and says more is available on the site.

## 3. Regions (16 main regions + Other)
Coins and settlements are organised by ancient region (region code = English name):
1. Lydia (lydia) — birthplace of coinage; Sardes the main mint. Croesus struck the first pure gold/silver coins.
2. Ionia (ionia) — Ephesus, Miletus, Smyrna, Phocaea, Chios, Samos.
3. Caria (caria) — Halicarnassus, Cnidus, Aphrodisias, Mylasa, Stratonicea, Tralles.
4. Lycia (lycia) — Xanthos, Patara, Myra, Phaselis; Lycian League coinage.
5. Phrygia (phrygia) — Apameia, Laodicea, Hierapolis, Synnada.
6. Mysia (mysia) — Cyzicus (electrum staters), Pergamum, Lampsacus, Adramyteum.
7. Bithynia (bithynia) — Nicomedia, Nicaea, Prusa, Heraclea Pontica.
8. Pamphylia (pamphylia) — Side, Aspendus, Perge, Attaleia.
9. Cilicia (cilicia) — Tarsus, Soloi, Celenderis, Anazarbus.
10. Cappadocia (cappadocia) — Caesarea (Mazaca), Tyana; drachms of the Cappadocian kings.
11. Galatia (galatia) — Ancyra (Ankara), Pessinus, Tavium.
12. Pisidia (pisidia) — Sagalassos, Cremna, Antioch in Pisidia, Selge, Termessos.
13. Troas (troas) — Abydus, Alexandria Troas, Ilium (Troy), Assos.
14. Paphlagonia (paphlagonia) — Sinope, Amastris, Pompeiopolis.
15. Aeolis (aeolis) — Cyme, Myrina, Aegae, Elaea.
16. Pontus (pontus) — Amisus, Amaseia, Neocaesarea; the age of Mithradates VI.
17. Other Ancient Regions (other) — Commagene, Isauria, Lycaonia, Sophene and border areas (Mesopotamia, Armenia, Thrace etc.).

## 4. How coin records are organised
Each coin "variant" record has: title (with catalogue reference, e.g. "Aspendus Imhoof-Blumer 1901-2 p. 316, 21"), region, metal (silver, gold, bronze, electrum, lead, iron), date range (BC as negative years: -400 = 400 BC), mint (Latin/English spelling: ephesus, sardes, cyzicus), authority (issuing king/emperor/city), obverse and reverse descriptions, weight/diameter when known, and watermarked images. Images are served from private storage with a watermark; high-resolution images are for Pro members. Sources: published catalogues and academic databases (citation given on each record).

## 5. Ancient settlement articles
There are TR and EN articles for 1,600+ ancient cities/sites. Each article covers history, location/region, archaeological remains, whether the city minted coins (has-coins) and links to coin records when available. Articles are listed by region category and marked on the interactive map; clicking a settlement on the map opens its article.

## 7. Coin recognition (AnatolianCoins app)
Photograph the obverse (and optionally the reverse) in the app; the AI performs a visual similarity search and lists the closest variants with confidence scores. Tips: plain background, direct light, coin filling the frame, sharp focus; adding both sides improves accuracy. The system is an identification aid; consult an expert for a definitive attribution. Uploaded images are not stored permanently (max 24 h for debugging).

## 8. Short numismatic glossary (~40 terms)
- **Coin**: metal money struck by a state/city authority with standard weight and type.
- **Obverse**: the main face, usually bearing a deity, king or emperor portrait.
- **Reverse**: the other face with a symbol, deity, inscription or civic emblem.
- **Legend**: the inscription on a coin; city name, ruler's titles, magistrate name.
- **Mint**: the city/workshop where the coin was struck.
- **Authority**: the king, emperor, dynast or city that issued the coin.
- **Die**: engraved steel/bronze stamp struck onto the blank; obverse and reverse dies.
- **Flan (blank)**: the prepared metal disc before striking.
- **Die axis**: the angle between obverse and reverse designs (given as clock hours).
- **Denomination**: the value unit (stater, drachm, obol etc.).
- **Stater**: basic gold/electrum/silver unit of the archaic-classical period.
- **Drachm**: the basic Greek silver unit; 6 obols.
- **Tetradrachm**: large silver coin worth 4 drachms.
- **Didrachm**: 2 drachms.
- **Hemidrachm**: half a drachm.
- **Obol**: one sixth of a drachm; small silver unit.
- **Electrum**: natural gold-silver alloy; metal of the first Lydian and Ionian coins.
- **Croeseid**: the first pure gold and silver coins struck by Croesus.
- **Cistophorus**: silver unit of Pergamene origin showing the cista mystica (after the 2nd c. BC).
- **Denarius**: the basic Roman silver unit.
- **Antoninianus**: 3rd-century AD Roman silver/billon unit, recognisable by the radiate crown.
- **Sestertius**: large Roman bronze/orichalcum unit.
- **As**: the basic Roman bronze unit.
- **Follis**: late Roman/Byzantine bronze unit.
- **Solidus**: late Roman/Byzantine gold unit.
- **Roman provincial coin (Greek imperial)**: bronze coins struck by Anatolian cities in their own name under Rome.
- **Autonomous coin**: civic issue with a city/deity image instead of an imperial portrait.
- **Countermark**: a small punch applied later to a circulating coin.
- **Incuse**: design sunk into the coin instead of in relief; archaic reverses show an incuse square.
- **Monogram**: a sign combining letters; abbreviation of a magistrate or mint.
- **Magistrate**: the civic official responsible for an issue; named in the legend.
- **Type**: the main pictorial subject (e.g. head of Athena / owl).
- **Variant**: a sub-version of a type differing in die, legend or symbol.
- **Hoard**: a group of coins buried/hidden together; important for dating.
- **Patina**: the oxide layer that forms on bronze coins over time.
- **Double strike**: the design struck twice because the flan shifted.
- **Overstrike**: striking a new design over an older coin.
- **Cast fake**: a modern imitation showing casting marks instead of die strike.
- **Grade**: degree of wear (e.g. VF, EF); NumisTR does not grade coins.
- **Weight standard**: regional weight systems such as Attic, Aeginetan, Persian, Rhodian.
- **Satrapal coinage**: coins struck by Persian satraps in Anatolia.
- **Dynast**: a local hereditary ruler (e.g. the Lycian dynasts).

## 9. Frequently asked questions (15)
1. **Is NumisTR paid?** Browsing is free. Only unlimited recognition and extra features in the app require Pro.
2. **How do I identify my coin?** Install the AnatolianCoins app, register for free, photograph the coin; the closest matches are listed. 10 scans per month are free.
3. **What is my coin worth?** NumisTR does not appraise or trade coins. Consult an auction house or expert for valuation.
4. **Can I buy or sell coins here?** No, NumisTR is not a marketplace.
5. **May I use the data and images?** Yes for education/research with attribution; images are watermarked and commercial use needs permission (see the Data Use Policy page).
6. **How do I get Pro?** Two ways: on the website at /en/plans (payment via iyzico) or in the app via a Google Play subscription. Price: €3.99/month or €34.99/year (Turkiye: 99.99 TL / 839.99 TL).
7. **How do I cancel?** If you subscribed on the website, at /en/my-account; if in the app, via Google Play > Subscriptions. Either way Pro continues until the end of the paid period.
8. **Is there an iOS version?** Currently Android (Google Play); iOS is planned.
9. **When does my scan quota reset?** On the 1st of each month.
10. **Which regions are covered?** The 16 main ancient Anatolian regions plus Other (see the region list).
11. **Where is the ancient map?** At /en/ancient-map; click a settlement to open its article.
12. **I am a student, can we collaborate?** Yes; students can apply for free Pro in the app, and for collaboration write to info@numistr.org.
13. **I found an error, how do I report it?** E-mail info@numistr.org with the record URL and a description.
14. **Do you match coin images?** Yes, the AI recognition in the app does; the website only offers search and browsing.
15. **Is there Turkish content?** Yes, all main content is in Turkish under /tr/.
